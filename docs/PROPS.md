# Props and materials

Every prop in the pubs is a proper low-poly model built in Blender from code, and every
surface uses a tiling PBR texture set (albedo, normal, roughness). Both are generated by
scripts, so they're easy to change and rebuild.

## Rebuilding

```sh
python3 tools/textures/build_textures.py        # textures (needs numpy + Pillow)
blender -b -P tools/blender/build_props.py       # models + catalogue (Blender 4.5 LTS)
godot --headless --path . --import               # reimport
```

Either step takes a list to rebuild only some sets or categories, such as
`build_textures.py wood labels` or `build_props.py -- chairs food`.

To look at the results (needs a renderer, so use xvfb in a container):

```sh
xvfb-run godot --path . --rendering-driver opengl3 --script res://tools/capture_props.gd -- out/   # contact sheet per category
xvfb-run godot --path . --rendering-driver opengl3 --script res://tools/capture_pubs.gd -- out/    # each pub, dressed
```

## The pieces

| File | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `tools/textures/build_textures.py` | Tiling 512 px texture sets in `assets/textures/materials/`, plus one-off prop textures in `assets/textures/props/` (the dartboard face, paintings, bottle labels, crisp packets, the chalkboard menu, drip mats) |
| `tools/blender/props_kit.py` | The modelling kit: boxes, bevels, lathes, rods, tori, labels, textured quads and discs, one bmesh per prop |
| `tools/blender/build_props.py` | Every prop, one function each, registered with its item kind and physics. Writes `assets/models/props/<category>.glb` and `catalogue.json` |
| `scripts/level/pub_materials.gd` | `PubMaterials`: the shared materials (`named("M_brass")`) and tinted architecture materials (`surface(set, colour)`). Triplanar, so nothing needs UVs |
| `scripts/level/prop_library.gd` | `PropLibrary`: loads the catalogue and models, swaps in the shared materials, and hands out visuals, collision shapes, masses and ready-made decor bodies |

## Conventions

- In Godot a model's origin is on the floor at its centre. Wall pieces have their back at
  local z = 0 and face +Z. Seats face -Z (the backrest is at +Z), matching `table_set`.
- Materials named `M_<name>` are swapped for `PubMaterials.named()`. `T_<texture>`
  materials carry a texture from `assets/textures/props/`. `C_<name>` materials are plain
  colours kept as exported (food, paint).
- `PropLibrary.resolve(kind, variant)` takes a variant name, an int, or a position. A
  position hashes to the same variant on every peer, so pubs look the same online.

## In the pubs

- `PubBuilder.material(colour)` returns textured materials for the era colours: wood grain
  on timber, trim, the bar and furniture; plaster (17th and 18th century) or wallpaper (19th)
  above the dado; plaster, raised panelling or glazed tiles on the dado; brick and stone round
  fireplaces; brushed metal on anything metallic. Floors are flagstones, oak boards or
  encaustic tiles. `PubEra` names the sets with `wall_tex` and `dado_tex`.
- `PubBuilder.prop()` stores a `variant` meta on every prop marker. Pickups
  (`PubItem.variant`) and placeholders use it, so one table's chairs all match.
- `table_set()` puts drinks at the real height of the chosen table and adds one to three bits
  of clutter (ashtrays, drip mats, candles, crisps, sauces, half-drunk glasses).
- `bar_counter()` lines the punters' side with clutter, including one each of the bell, tip
  jar, pickled eggs, ice bucket, pump clip and menu. `back_bar()` fills its shelves with real
  bottles and glasses, pinned until the physics knocks them off.
- `dartboard()` and `pool_table()` use the models. The dartboard root is in group
  `dartboards` with a `facing` meta for the darts game.
- `PubLevel` calls `PubBuilder.dress()` after `_build()`. It stands up to 11 pieces of
  furniture (always trying the piano first, with its stool) against the walls and hangs
  pictures, mirrors and shelves, on the ground floor and along galleries. It keeps clear of
  doors, windows, fixtures, beams, stairs, the bar, the oche, every marker and the landing
  under every open ledge.

## Physics

`PropLibrary.spawn(name)` makes a decor prop as a `RigidBody3D` in group `pub_props`, with
the catalogue's mass and shape, asleep until something hits it. Meta: `prop`, `kind`,
`fragile` (glass and crockery), `pinned` (wall pieces and shelf bottles start frozen until
knocked loose). Big pieces are on layers world and props (1 | 128) so they block fighters;
small ones are on props (128) only. Mask is 1 | 8 | 128. `PubBuilder.decor()` names them
`D<n>` in build order under `World/Decor`. The physics workstream owns how they are knocked
about and synced, and uses `PropLibrary.visual()`, `shape()` and `mass()` for furniture.
